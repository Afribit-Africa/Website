Title: Building Local Economic Intelligence with Bitcoin Payments
Afribit and Insats are testing a new way to understand local prices without forcing merchants to change how they already do business.
In our first live payment test, Sam Designs received Bitcoin through an Afribit payment address connected to the merchant’s own Blink wallet. The payment moved from the public payment address to the merchant-controlled wallet, while Afribit and Insats observed the transaction event for research purposes. This proved an important foundation: payments can be tracked for settlement evidence without Afribit taking custody of the merchant’s funds.
But a payment alone does not tell the full story.
A transaction can show that value moved. It can show the merchant, the time, the payment rail, and the amount. What it cannot reliably show is what was bought. For Insats, that missing layer matters. Our goal is to understand everyday purchasing power: how much bread, milk, cooking oil, vegetables, airtime, transport, and household goods cost in local currency and in sats over time.
That is why the next layer is microtasking.
After a payment event, Insats can ask a simple follow-up question: what did this payment buy? A buyer, merchant, or trusted contributor can answer through a lightweight mobile experience. The answer can be typed, selected from common goods, or spoken by voice. The system then helps standardize the information into useful economic data: item, quantity, local measure, brand or type where relevant, price, and payment context.
This keeps the merchant workflow light. The merchant can keep accepting Bitcoin or M-Pesa as usual, while Insats builds a cleaner picture of local prices from verified moments of trade.
The third layer is an Afribit POS experience.
For merchants who want a smoother checkout tool, Afribit can provide a simple mobile PWA: enter a customer phone number, enter or speak the items sold, trigger an M-Pesa STK push, confirm payment, and settle the equivalent value to the merchant in Bitcoin. This would allow local businesses to accept familiar M-Pesa payments while receiving sats, with Insats collecting structured price information at the point of sale.
The product direction is simple:
- merchants should not be forced into complicated tools;
- payments should remain linked to merchant-controlled wallets wherever possible;
- price data should be collected with consent and reviewed before becoming public;
- Bitcoin should be used not only as money, but as a measurement layer for local purchasing power.
This is still an early pilot. Current work focuses on Sam Designs and a small group of merchants before expanding further. The goal is not to replace existing business habits overnight. The goal is to build rails that quietly support them, while producing better local economic intelligence for communities, builders, and organizations.
Afribit provides the payment and circular economy rails. Insats turns local trade into a living price index.
Together, they can help answer one practical question:
How far does money go here, today, in shillings and in sats?
